## 2. Why now (the trends)

1. **Regulation is forcing transparency.** The CMA's final report on vet services (24 March 2026) found weak competition and high prices from poor consumer information, and ordered price, treatment and prescription transparency, with some measures phasing in to 2027, and urged reform of the Veterinary Surgeons Act. Tools that help owners act earlier and choose cheaper routes (online consults) fit that direction.
2. **Insurers are squeezed.** UK premiums fell through 2026 on price competition (Defaqto) while claim costs keep rising (reported about 20% higher claim costs). Margins only recover through **lower claims and better retention**, which is exactly what Petra sells.
3. **The US shows the growth path.** US insured pets reached about 7m at end of 2025, with record quarterly premiums of $1.53bn in Q1 2026 (+16.8%), yet only about 4% of US pets are insured. A prevention-plus-rewards product travels.
4. **Wearables are mainstream.** The global pet wearables market is estimated at **$3bn to $7bn in 2025**, growing about **11% to 15% a year**, with smart collars the largest segment (estimates vary by publisher).
5. **Telematics proved the model in car insurance.** Co-op reported young drivers with black boxes were **20% less likely to crash** and their claims **30% cheaper**; Aviva reported new drivers on telematics cut collision claims by nearly **50%** vs about 30% on standard cover. (Insurer data; self-selection caveat.)
6. **Shared-value insurance works in human health.** Vitality reports a **4% claims cost reduction with 180% ROI** in an employer study, and RAND Europe found a **34%** activity increase when rewards were linked to a wearable. Incentives change behaviour.

## 3. Academic evidence (keep claims modest)

- **Lifespan:** McMillan et al. (2024), *Scientific Reports*: about 585,000 UK dogs, median lifespan about 12.5 years, large breed differences. Petra's healthspan stop uses this.
- **Lean dogs live longer:** Kealy et al. (2002), *JAVMA*: Labradors kept lean lived a median of about **1.8 years longer**. Supports walk targets and weight focus.
- **Wearables detect change early (preliminary):** a 2025 *Animals* case series of dogs with osteoarthritis wearing a smart collar found that in one case **activity fell before the owner noticed**; small sample, authors call for validation. A Waltham/Kinship study trained collar accelerometer models against owner reports for over 10,000 dogs. Say: "early evidence, which our pilot is designed to test."

## 4. Commercial model (car telematics, applied to pets)

| Car telematics | Petra |
|---|---|
| Pay How You Drive | **Pay How You Care**: personal walk target + weekly check-ins = Care Score = discount |
| Young-driver black box | **First-time and young owners** get a lower entry price that keeps dropping |
| Bonus miles | **Bonus care**: free online consults and nurse checks |
| Careful drivers self-select | **Careful owners opt in**: better risk pool for the insurer |
| Crash data, faster claims | **Early warnings** + owner-shared history for faster, less disputed claims |
| Stolen car tracking | **Lost-pet GPS** cuts loss and theft payouts |
| Box, then phone app | **Phone first**, Walk Companion optional and insurer-subsidised |

Lessons applied: discount-only (no surcharges or cancellations), location never leaves the phone, personal fair targets, Rest mode during illness.

## 5. The numbers (from the model, Base scenario)

### For one insurer with 100,000 policies

| Line | Per year | Per engaged member |
|---|---|---|
| Engaged members (30%) | 30,000 | |
| Claims avoided (12% of claims cost) | **£965k** | £32.17 |
| Retention value (+5 pts: margin + avoided acquisition cost) | **£237k** | £7.89 |
| Petra platform fee (£1 per member per month) | (£360k) | (£12.00) |
| Discounts paid to members (avg 4% of £389) | (£467k) | (£15.56) |
| Petra outcome share (15% of claims avoided) | (£145k) | (£4.82) |
| **Net benefit to insurer + underwriter** | **£230k** | **£7.67** |
| Return on cost | **1.24x** | |
| **Break-even claims reduction** | **8.6%** | |

**How to say it:** "If engaged members' claims fall by more than about 9%, the insurer is better off, and every point above that is profit. Car telematics insurers report 30% cheaper claims for young drivers."

**Downside is negative** (5% claims reduction, 6% average discount). Mitigation in the product: discount tiers are funded from measured savings and reconciled against a control group, so if the savings don't appear, the discount pool shrinks. That is the honest answer if a judge asks.

## 7. Q&A bank (who answers)

**Business (C)**
- *Why would an insurer pay?* Break-even at about 9% fewer claims among engaged members; telematics insurers report 30% cheaper claims; plus retention and acquisition. Discount pool is reconciled against a control group.
- *What if claims don't fall?* Then the outcome share is zero and discount tiers are funded only from measured savings; the pilot's control group tells us within 12 weeks to 6 months.
- *Isn't 12% optimistic?* It's our base assumption, labelled. Downside 5% is negative, which is why pricing is outcome-linked. Vitality's human-health evidence ranges from 4% to 15%.
- *Who's the first customer?* An insurer already working with Doctapet, targeting first-time and young owners of dogs, where claims value concentrates (dogs are about 76% of claims value, ABI).
- *How big can this get?* UK TAM about £150m; Base 2031 revenue £11.6m with about 8% of UK insured policies engaged; then US and Europe.

**Product and AI (A)**
- *What if the AI is wrong?* Deterministic red-flag rules run first; AI can escalate but never downgrade; risky questions skip the AI and go to a vet; never diagnoses or doses.
- *Does the movement check really work?* It runs a pose model on video; today it's demonstrated on sample clips, and live analysis of owner videos is part of the pilot. (Say exactly what the build does.)
- *Why not just a collar company?* Collars exist; nobody connects the data to the premium, the vet and the claim. Phone-first means no hardware barrier.

**Regulation and fairness (A or C)**
- *Is it legal?* No diagnosis or prescribing (Veterinary Surgeons Act 1966); vets via Doctapet's regulated consults; FCA Consumer Duty: discount-only, clear, fair value; UK GDPR: location stays on device, granular consent, delete-all.
- *Doesn't it penalise old or disabled pets?* No: targets are personal to breed, age and health; Rest mode protects the discount; discounts reward care, not health outcomes.
- *Could owners game it?* Discounts are capped and tied to consistent patterns over weeks; the Walk Companion corroborates; gaming still produces more walking.
- *What does the underwriter see?* Engagement and tier per policy; anonymised group trends; never routes, raw video or individual health answers for pricing.

**Market (B)**
- *Competitors?* Telehealth (Joii, FirstVet), collars (Tractive and others), insurer wellness add-ons. None closes the loop from care to premium to vet to claim.
- *Why now?* CMA vet market remedies (March 2026), insurers squeezed by falling premiums and rising claims costs, and a dog ownership boom (15.5m dogs).
